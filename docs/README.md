# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

This repository centralizes the core project management processes used by OctoAcme. It is designed to help new teammates and stakeholders quickly understand the team’s operating model, roles, communication rhythm, and quality expectations across the project lifecycle.

## Process Summary

OctoAcme follows a lightweight, customer-first project lifecycle: initiation to validate the business need and align stakeholders; planning to turn ideas into a scoped backlog, milestone map, and definition of done; execution to deliver work in small, testable increments; release and deployment to ship changes safely; and close/retrospective to capture lessons learned and convert them into improvement actions. The project framework emphasizes clear ownership, measurable outcomes, and iterative delivery rather than rigid process overhead.

Across the lifecycle, the team uses a consistent set of artifacts to keep work aligned: a project charter or one-pager, backlog with acceptance criteria, risk register, milestone plan, and retrospective notes. These artifacts help ensure that work stays connected to customer value, dependencies are visible, and decisions are transparent to stakeholders and delivery teams.

## Roles and Responsibilities

The OctoAcme model defines clear personas so responsibilities are easy to understand and hand off. Product managers define the problem, priorities, and success metrics. Project managers coordinate planning, schedules, communication, and risk or dependency management. Developers design, build, and test software to meet acceptance criteria, while QA and testing functions validate quality and readiness for release. Stakeholders provide context, approvals, and feedback throughout the project lifecycle.

## Communication and Risk Management

OctoAcme relies on a regular cadence of standups, weekly syncs, and milestone-based stakeholder updates to keep delivery moving and issues visible. Daily standups focus on progress, blockers, and dependencies; weekly project reviews surface risks, decisions, and next steps; and escalations follow a clear path from the team to the project manager, product lead, and sponsor when business-impacting issues require broader action. The team also maintains a risk register to track impact, likelihood, ownership, and mitigation steps so that cross-team dependencies and delivery risks are monitored continuously.

## Quality Assurance and Delivery Practices

Quality is built into project execution rather than treated as a final checkpoint. New work is expected to include clear acceptance criteria, automated testing where appropriate, CI checks for linting and security, and smoke tests for critical workflows before release. Release processes add additional safeguards such as staging verification, rollback planning, release notes, and post-deploy checks to reduce operational risk. After each sprint or milestone, the team runs a retrospective to capture what went well, what should improve, and which action items need clear owners and due dates.
